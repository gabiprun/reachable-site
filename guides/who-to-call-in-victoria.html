Who to call in Victoria

Greater Victoria has dozens of organisations that will help you for free. The hard part is knowing which one. This page sorts them by what you need — not by what they call themselves.

Checked on 3 September 2026

At a glance

If you are in danger right now
Call 911. If you are thinking about suicide, call or text 9-8-8, any hour of any day.
If you do not know who to call
Dial 2-1-1. A real person searches for you and gives you the name, number and hours.
What this page is
A list of Victoria organisations grouped by what you need: food, forms, someone to talk to, help at home, legal help.
What it costs
Almost everything here is free. Where there is a fee, we say so.
The hardest part
The first sentence on the phone. We wrote one out for you further down.

If it is urgent, start here

You will not be sent an ambulance for calling a crisis line

People stay silent because they are afraid of what happens next. The crisis line's job is to talk with you. They only involve emergency services if your life is in immediate danger, and they tell you if they are going to.

If you do not know who to call

This is the most common situation, and there is a number for exactly it.

211 — free, confidential, and available in many languages. Tell them what is wrong; they find the service and give you the number and the hours.

2-1-1

Dial or text 2-1-1. Phone and chat are staffed 9 am to 9 pm on weekdays. The directory at bc.211.ca works any time.

A "no" letter is not the end

Most benefit refusals can be challenged, and there is usually a deadline measured in weeks. Take the letter to TAPS or Disability Alliance BC the same week it arrives. Do not wait until you feel ready.

I am being treated unfairly because of my disability

In BC it is against the law to refuse someone housing, a job, or a service because of a disability. That includes refusing to make a reasonable change so you can use the service — a ramp, a different appointment time, a form read out loud, a service animal allowed inside.

You do not need to be certain it was discrimination before you phone. That is what these people work out with you.

There is a time limit

Human rights complaints in BC must be filed within a set time after the event. Do not spend months deciding whether it was "bad enough". Phone the clinic and let them tell you.

What to say when you phone

The hardest part of asking for help is the first ten seconds, when you do not know the words for what you need. You do not have to know. Here is a script that works at almost any of these organisations.

If you do not know what to ask for

"Hello. I'm not sure if you're the right place, and I'm not sure what this is called. Here's what's going on: [say the plain fact — I have no food until Thursday / I got a letter saying my assistance is cut off / I can't get up the stairs any more / I haven't spoken to anyone in a week]. Can you tell me who I should be talking to?"

Three things to know about that script:

  • "I'm not sure if you're the right place" is a good opening, not a weak one. It tells the person their first job is to route you, and they are good at that.
  • Say the plain fact, not the category. "I have no food until Thursday" gets a faster answer than "I'd like to enquire about food security supports."
  • Ask for a name and a direct number before you hang up. Then, if the call drops or the transfer fails, you are not starting over.

If you are handed on to someone else

"Before you transfer me — can I have your name, and the number I've reached you at, in case we get cut off? And who am I being transferred to?"

If your hearing, your voice or your nerves make phone calls hard, most of these organisations take email, and many will accept a call from a friend or family member on your behalf. Say so at the start of the call.

Why people never make the call

  • "Someone else needs it more than me." These services are funded to be used. An empty chair does not go to someone else.
  • They do not know the word. "Advocacy" is the word for someone who argues with the government on your behalf, for free. Almost nobody knows that, so almost nobody asks for it.
  • One bad experience years ago. Staff change, programmes change, funding changes. A closed door in 2019 is not a closed door now.
  • They tried the main number, got voicemail, and stopped. Leave the message. Most of these organisations are small and do call back.
  • They think it will cost money. Everything in the tables above is free, except the recreation and taxi programmes, which are cheap by design.
  • They are ashamed. Nobody at Our Place, TAPS or the Disability Resource Centre thinks less of anyone who walks in. Many of the staff have been in the same chair.

If none of this fits

211 — the fallback for everything. Free, confidential, and they will find the service even when you cannot name it.

2-1-1

Or call the Reachable Line. We will look it up with you, out loud, at your pace, and we will stay on the line while you decide who to phone next.

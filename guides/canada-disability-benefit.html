The Canada Disability Benefit: up to $204.20 a month

A monthly payment from the federal government for people aged 18 to 64 who live with a disability and have a low or modest income. It is new — the first payments went out in July 2025 — and a lot of people who qualify have still not applied.

What the Canada Disability Benefit is

The Canada Disability Benefit — people say CDB — is a monthly payment from Service Canada for working-age adults with disabilities who have low or modest incomes.

It is not a loan and not a tax refund. It is money paid to you every month, on top of anything you already get. The first payments went out in July 2025, so it is still new enough that many people have never heard of it.

You need the Disability Tax Credit first

You cannot get the Canada Disability Benefit without being approved for the Disability Tax Credit. There is no way around it, no exceptions, and no separate medical form for the CDB.

So if you do not have the Disability Tax Credit yet, that is your first job. It is a form called T2201, your doctor or nurse practitioner fills in part of it, and there is no income test. Our Disability Tax Credit guide walks through the whole thing, including what to say at the appointment.

If you had the Disability Tax Credit once and the approval ran out, the CDB stops with it. Reapply for the credit and the benefit can start again.

Good news if you are applying for the credit now

Starting in fall 2026, there is a one-time $150 payment to help with the cost of getting the Disability Tax Credit certificate filled in — the fee some practitioners charge. You do not have to apply for it.

Who qualifies

All of these have to be true:

  • You are between 18 and 64.
  • You are approved for the Disability Tax Credit.
  • You live in Canada for tax purposes.
  • You are a Canadian citizen, a permanent resident, a protected person, a person registered or entitled to be registered under the Indian Act, or a temporary resident who has lived in Canada for the past 18 months.
  • You — and your spouse or common-law partner, if you have one — filed last year's tax return.

People serving a federal sentence of two years or more are not eligible while they are in prison.

The tax return is not optional

The whole benefit is worked out from your tax return, so an unfiled return means no payment — even if you had no income at all. If you and your partner are behind on filing, get that done first. Volunteers in Victoria will do it for free, including past years.

How much you get

The most you can get is set for a 12-month period that runs from July to June, and it is worked out from your tax return from the year before.

The amount is adjusted for inflation each year.

Those are the maximum amounts. The payment gets smaller once your adjusted family net income passes a certain point. How high that point is depends on your own situation — whether you have a partner, whether your partner also gets the CDB, and whether either of you has income from work. There is an estimator on the Government of Canada's CDB page, and the phone number below will work it out with you. Check the current amount for your case rather than guessing.

Working does not automatically disqualify you

Some of what you earn is ignored completely. For July 2026 to June 2027, the first $10,210 of a single person's working income is set aside before the benefit is reduced, or $14,294 for a couple's combined working income. If you have a job and assumed you earn too much, apply anyway and let them do the math.

How to apply, step by step

  1. Get the Disability Tax Credit. Nothing else can happen first. See our guide.
  2. Make sure last year's tax return is filed — yours and your partner's. Free clinics in Victoria will do it, including years you have missed.
  3. Check your mail. Service Canada sent letters to many people who already have the Disability Tax Credit. The letter has a 6-digit application code. If you have one, applying online with that code and your Social Insurance Number is the fastest route.
  4. No letter? Apply anyway. You do not need the code. You can apply online, by phone at 1-833-486-3007, in person at a Service Canada office, or by mailing the paper form.
  5. Have these ready: your Social Insurance Number, your status in Canada, your net income from line 23600 of your last notice of assessment, and your bank details for direct deposit. Having them speeds the whole thing up.
  6. If someone applies for you — a guardian, trustee, or someone with power of attorney — they bring a copy of the legal document and their own ID.
  7. Watch for the decision, then watch the third Thursday. Payments land on the third Thursday of the month.

The mailing address for a paper form is: Service Canada Centre, CDB Processing Centre, P.O. Box 60, Boucherville QC J4B 5E6.

What to say when Service Canada answers

"I'd like to apply for the Canada Disability Benefit. I'm approved for the Disability Tax Credit. I didn't get a letter with an application code, but I understand I can still apply. I have my SIN and my last notice of assessment here. Can you also check whether I'm owed any back pay?"

Back pay and when money arrives

Your first payment comes on the third Thursday of the month after you are approved, and any back pay comes with it.

Back pay can go up to 24 months from the day your application is received — but never for months before June 2025, because that is when the benefit started.

That is the part worth acting on today. Every month you wait is a month that can eventually fall off the back end of that 24-month window. If you have been putting the application off, this is the reason not to.

Does it affect my BC disability assistance?

This is the question we hear most, and it deserves a straight answer rather than a guess.

The Canada Disability Benefit is a federal payment. BC disability assistance (the PWD designation) is a provincial one. They are separate programs, run by different governments, and the CDB does not replace your provincial cheque.

What we will not do is promise you how the province treats the money, because that is a provincial decision and it is not ours to state. Ask before you assume, and ask someone whose job it is:

  • Disability Alliance BC — advocates who work with the federal and provincial systems together every day. 1-800-663-1278
  • Together Against Poverty Society (TAPS) in Victoria — they handle income assistance questions and appeals. 250-361-3521

Do not let the uncertainty stop you from asking. Being unsure is a reason to phone, not a reason to skip a benefit you qualify for.

Why people miss this

  • They do not have the Disability Tax Credit, or theirs lapsed and nobody told them the CDB would stop with it.
  • They or their partner did not file a tax return. No return, no payment — even with no income.
  • "I earn too much." Thousands of dollars of working income are ignored, and the cut-off is higher than most people expect. Apply and find out.
  • No letter arrived, so they assumed they could not apply. You can. The letter and its code are a shortcut, not a requirement.
  • They are on BC disability assistance and assumed this replaces it. It does not. It is a separate federal payment.
  • It is new. The benefit only started in 2025, and word has not reached everyone it should.

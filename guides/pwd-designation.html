Getting the PWD designation in BC

PWD is British Columbia's disability designation. It brings a monthly payment, a free bus pass, help with dental work and glasses, and the right to earn money from a job without losing your cheque. The form is long, and plenty of people are turned down the first time. That is not the end of it.

Checked on 3 September 2026

At a glance

What you get
For a single person: $983.50 a month for support, plus up to $500 a month toward rent, plus $52 a month for transportation or a free BC bus pass. On top of that: dental, glasses, medical equipment, free prescriptions, and the right to earn $16,200 a year from work.
Who it is for
Adults 18 and over living in BC (you can start at 17 and a half) who have a severe physical or mental impairment expected to last more than two years, and who have low income and few assets.
What it costs
Nothing to apply. Free advocates in Victoria will fill the form in with you and will take an appeal for you if you are refused.
How long it takes
The ministry gives you about three months to return the form once it sends it to you. Most of that time goes on doctor and assessor appointments, so book them early. The ministry does not publish how long its own decision takes — ask when you send the form in.
Not the same as
The federal Disability Tax Credit. They are two different programs with two different forms. See our Disability Tax Credit guide — most people should apply for both.

What PWD is

PWD stands for Persons with Disabilities. It is a status you apply for from the BC Ministry of Social Development and Poverty Reduction. Once the ministry gives you the designation, you receive disability assistance: a payment that arrives every month.

Disability assistance pays more than regular income assistance, lets you keep far more of what you earn from a job, and switches on a list of other things — a bus pass, dental work, glasses, free prescriptions, help with equipment like a walker or a wheelchair.

The designation is the door. Almost everything else on this page is behind it.

PWD is not the Disability Tax Credit

These two get mixed up more than anything else we are asked about, so here it is side by side.

Two different programs, two different forms
PWD designationDisability Tax Credit
Who runs itThe BC governmentThe federal government (Canada Revenue Agency)
What it gives youMoney in your bank account every monthA credit that lowers income tax you owe
Income and asset testYes. Assets up to $100,000 for a single personNo income test at all
The formPWD Designation Application (form HR2883) — three partsForm T2201 — two parts
What it unlocksBus pass, dental, glasses, equipment, free prescriptionsThe RDSP and the Canada Disability Benefit

You can have both, and most people should

Having one does not give you the other, and being refused for one does not mean you will be refused for the other — they use completely different tests. If you qualify for PWD there is a good chance you also qualify for the Disability Tax Credit, which then opens the RDSP and the Canada Disability Benefit. Apply for both.

What it pays every month

These are the ministry's rates, effective 1 December 2025. Support is for food and daily costs. Shelter is for rent and utilities, and is paid up to the maximum based on what you actually pay.

Everyone with PWD also gets a $52 a month transportation supplement. You can take it as cash, or trade it for a free BC bus pass — see what else comes with PWD.

So a single person with PWD who pays $500 or more in rent receives $1,535.50 a month in total.

Working while on PWD

A lot of people turn down shifts because they are frightened of losing the cheque. Here is the actual rule.

In 2026 a single person with PWD can earn $16,200 in a year from work before their assistance is reduced at all. A couple where one person has PWD can earn $23,400. A couple where both have PWD can earn $32,400.

This is a yearly amount, not a monthly one, so you can have a busy month and a quiet month. But anything you do not use by the end of the year is gone — it does not roll over.

You must report the money

If you earn anything, you have to send in a monthly report (form HR0081) by the 5th of the month. Miss it and your payment can be held. Report even a small amount — the exemption only protects you if the ministry knows about the income.

Who qualifies

There are two separate tests, and you have to pass both.

The money test. You must have little income, and assets under the limit: $100,000 for a single person or a couple where one has PWD, and $200,000 where both have PWD. Your home does not count. Neither does one vehicle, an RDSP, or certain trusts.

The disability test. All of these must be true:

  • You are 18 or older. You can start the application at 17 and a half.
  • You have a severe physical or mental impairment.
  • A doctor or nurse practitioner expects it to last more than two years.
  • It significantly restricts your daily living activities — things like cooking, shopping, managing money, getting around, keeping yourself clean, taking your medication.
  • Because of that, you need help from another person, an assistive device, or an assistance animal.

The rules look at what your condition does to your daily life, not at your diagnosis. Two people with the same condition can get different answers, because the form is asking how much of your day it takes away from you.

The form, and who fills in what

The PWD Designation Application (form HR2883) has three sections, and three different people fill them in.

The three parts of the PWD application
SectionWhat it isWho fills it in
Section 1Your own account of how your condition affects youYou
Section 2The medical reportYour doctor or a nurse practitioner
Section 3The assessor reportA doctor, nurse practitioner or other prescribed professional — a registered nurse, social worker, occupational therapist and others count

Section 3 is the one people underestimate. The assessor report is about your daily life, and a social worker or occupational therapist who has watched you struggle with the stairs can often say more useful things than a doctor who sees you for ten minutes twice a year. Choose your assessor as carefully as you choose your words.

Describe a bad day, not a good day

This is the single most common mistake. People fill the form in on a day they are coping, and they write about that day, because they were raised not to complain. The ministry then reads a form that says you manage fine.

Write about a typical bad day. How long does it take to get dressed when it is bad? What did you stop doing? What did somebody else have to do for you? If pain or fatigue comes and goes, say how many days a week it wins.

How to apply, step by step

  1. Show you qualify financially first. Apply for assistance online at My Self Serve (myselfserve.gov.bc.ca) or call 1-866-866-0800. Have your ID, Social Insurance Number, rent and utility costs and bank balance ready.
  2. Ask the ministry for the PWD Designation Application. Say those words. It is a separate form from the assistance application, and nobody will send it to you unless you ask.
  3. Call a free advocate before you write anything. TAPS in Victoria and Disability Alliance BC do this all day, every day. They know which words the ministry is looking for. This one phone call is the biggest single thing you can do to get approved.
  4. Book the doctor and the assessor appointments straight away. You have about three months to return the form, and the wait for appointments is usually what eats the time.
  5. Fill in Section 1 yourself, about a bad day. Take your time. Write on scrap paper first if it helps.
  6. Take the form to your doctor or nurse practitioner for Section 2, and to your prescribed professional for Section 3. Give them a copy of what you wrote in Section 1 so their answers match yours.
  7. Photocopy or photograph every page before you send it. If you are refused, you will need to know exactly what the ministry read.
  8. Send it back and write down the date you sent it. Ask them to confirm they received it.

What to say when you phone the ministry

"I want to apply for the Persons with Disabilities designation. Can you please send me the PWD Designation Application? I'd also like to know what I need to do to show I qualify financially, and how I send the form back when it's done."

What to say when you phone an advocate

"I'm applying for the PWD designation and I'd like help with the form. Can someone go through it with me before I send it in? I can come to your office, or do it over the phone if that's easier."

If they say no

Read this part even if you have just sent your form in. A refusal letter is the moment people give up, and the clock starts the day it arrives.

A "no" from the ministry is a first answer, not a final one. There are two more stages after it, both free, and free advocates in Victoria will do both of them with you.

Stage one: reconsideration

  • You have 20 business days from getting the decision to ask for a reconsideration. Ask for the Request for Reconsideration form at your ministry office or on 1-866-866-0800.
  • Reconsideration is not just a complaint. You can add new evidence — a letter from a specialist, a fuller assessor report, a page in your own words about what the first form left out. This is where most refusals are worth fighting, because usually the problem was missing evidence, not your health.
  • The ministry mails you the reconsideration decision within 10 business days of receiving your form.

Stage two: the tribunal

  • If the reconsideration is still a no, you can appeal to the Employment and Assistance Appeal Tribunal. It is independent of the ministry.
  • You file a Notice of Appeal (form EAAT001). The tribunal's own rule since 13 May 2024 is 20 business days from the reconsideration decision. Some government pages still say seven business days. Do not gamble on which is right — send it the same week the letter arrives.
  • The hearing happens within 15 business days of the tribunal receiving your form. You can have it in person, by phone, by video, or in writing. You will normally get the decision within about 10 business days of the hearing.
  • The tribunal is free. Call 1-866-557-0035 if you are stuck on the form.

People do win these

In the tribunal's 2023 to 2024 year, PWD designation was its biggest single subject — 131 files. Of 375 ministry appeals heard that year, the tribunal overturned the ministry in 69 of them. And the tribunal is the last stage — reconsideration comes first, and that is where you get to add the evidence the original form was missing.

Nobody publishes a figure for how many refusals are reversed at reconsideration. What we can tell you is that TAPS and Disability Alliance BC run free services that exist for exactly this, and they do not run them because it never works. Do not stop at the first no.

What to say the day the refusal letter arrives

"I got a letter turning down my PWD application, dated [the date on the letter]. I want to request a reconsideration. Please send me the Request for Reconsideration form today, and tell me the date it has to be back by."

Then call TAPS at 250-361-3521 and read them the letter.

What else comes with PWD

The monthly cheque is not the whole of it. Once you have the designation, ask about all of these.

  • A free bus pass. Your $52 transportation supplement pays for a yearly BC Transit pass, good on Victoria buses and handyDART. Or keep the $52 in cash and buy your own fares — you can switch either way by telling the ministry before the 5th of the month. Ask online at myselfserve.gov.bc.ca/BusPass or on 1-866-866-0800. Allow up to 6 weeks for it to arrive.
  • Dental work — up to $1,000 over two calendar years, plus emergency dental to stop pain.
  • Eye exams and glasses — an exam every two years, and glasses at the ministry's rate.
  • Free prescriptions. Being on disability assistance puts you on PharmaCare Plan C, which covers 100% of eligible drugs and the dispensing fee. It is automatic.
  • Medical equipment — canes, walkers, wheelchairs, hospital beds, lifts and more, with a prescription and usually a therapist's assessment.
  • Diet supplements of $15 to $80 a month depending on the diet, and a Monthly Nutritional Supplement of up to $180 plus $45 a month for people with a severe deteriorating condition.
  • Medical travel for essential treatment you cannot get nearby.
  • Crisis supplements when something unexpected happens: up to $50 per person per month for food, up to $110 per person a year for clothing, and help with rent or a broken essential.

Get approval before you buy anything

This is the rule that costs people the most money. For equipment, travel and most supplements, the ministry has to approve it before you spend. Buy the walker first and you may be told no afterwards. Call 1-866-866-0800 and ask them to confirm it in advance.

Our guide to help paying for dentists, prescriptions and glasses goes through all of this in detail.

If you do not meet the PWD test: PPMB

There is a second designation almost nobody hears about: Persons with Persistent Multiple Barriers, or PPMB. It is for people on income assistance who have a long-term health condition and other serious barriers to finding work.

It pays less than PWD but more than regular income assistance — $610 a month support for a single person instead of $560 — and it lets you earn $1,080 a month before your cheque is cut, instead of $600. It also brings health and dental supplements, and it takes away the requirement to look for work.

The barriers the ministry counts include having been homeless in the last 12 months, leaving school before Grade 12, needing English language training, a criminal record, having used emergency, mental health or addiction services several times in the past year, arriving as a refugee in the last two years, or having been a child in care.

You can hold PPMB while your PWD application is being prepared or appealed. Ask the ministry for form HR2892.

Why people miss this

  • "I was refused CPP Disability, so I won't get PWD." Different programs, different rules, different decision-makers. A no from one says nothing about the other.
  • A doctor said they "might not qualify" and they never applied. Doctors fill in one of three sections. They do not make the decision.
  • They described their best day on the form. See the warning in the form section — this is the big one.
  • They gave up after the first refusal. There are two more free stages and free advocates for both.
  • They did not know they can start at 17 and a half.
  • They never asked for the health supplements, so they paid for their own glasses and dental work for years.
  • They turned down work because they thought any earnings would cut their cheque. $16,200 a year says otherwise.
  • They confused PWD with the Disability Tax Credit, applied for one, and assumed they were done.

Where to get help in Victoria

Ministry of Social Development and Poverty Reduction — to apply, to ask for the PWD form, or to ask about a supplement

1-866-866-0800

But before you phone the ministry, phone one of these. They are free, they are local, and this is what they do.

  • Together Against Poverty Society (TAPS), 828 View Street — free legal advocacy for PWD applications, refusals, reconsiderations and appeals. Walk in Monday, Tuesday, Thursday and Friday, 9 am to noon and 1 pm to 4 pm; Wednesday is phone only. 250-361-3521
  • Victoria Disability Resource Centre, 817A Fort Street — help filling in benefit forms, peer support, accessible parking permits. Monday to Thursday 9 am to 4 pm, Friday 9 am to 2 pm. 250-595-0044
  • Disability Alliance BC, Advocacy Access — province-wide help with PWD applications and appeals, mostly by phone, so you do not need to travel. 1-800-663-1278
  • Community Legal Assistance Society — a free non-profit law firm that takes benefit denial cases. 1-888-685-6222
  • Employment and Assistance Appeal Tribunal — for help with the Notice of Appeal form. 1-866-557-0035
  • 211 — dial 211 any time for other options near you.

Or call the Reachable Line and we will look it up with you, out loud, at your pace.

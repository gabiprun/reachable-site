I have no money at all

If you already get income or disability assistance

Ask for a crisis supplement. It is a one-time payment. It can cover food, clothing, rent, a utility bill, a bed, or an emergency repair.

You have to pass three tests. Say all three out loud when you phone:

  1. It was unexpected. Something happened you could not plan for.
  2. You have no other money. No cash, nothing you can sell or borrow.
  3. Going without is dangerous. Your health, or a child's safety, is at risk.

Most people are refused the first time because they never say the third part. Say it plainly.

What to say when you phone 1-866-866-0800

"I would like to apply for a crisis supplement, please. It is for [food / my rent / my hydro bill / a bed].

This was unexpected — [what happened].

I have no money and nothing I can sell or borrow to cover it.

If I go without, my health will be in immediate danger, because [say why: no food, no heat, no fridge for medicine, no bed to sleep in].

Can you please put this request through and tell me the decision?"

If they say no

"Thank you. Can I have that refusal in writing, with the reason?

And I would like to ask for a reconsideration. Can you tell me how to do that and the date it is due by?"

Then phone TAPS at 250-361-3521. Advocates there do this every day.

Rough amounts, set 1 August 2026: food up to $50 per person per month; clothing up to $110 per person per year; shelter up to your family's support-and-shelter rate, normally once a year. Beds, repairs and utilities are decided case by case.

If you were refused assistance, or are still waiting

Ask for hardship assistance instead. Same phone number.

It is for people who do not qualify for regular assistance but have no other way to eat or stay housed. It is paid one month at a time, and you re-apply each month.

It exists if you are waiting on EI, CPP, Old Age Security, WorkSafe or veterans' money; if you are on strike; if you have no ID yet; if you are waiting for a Social Insurance Number; or if you simply need food or shelter right now. Some kinds have to be paid back later. Ask which kind you are being given.

Do not borrow first

One of the three tests is that you have no other money. If you borrow from a friend or a payday lender before you ask, the ministry can say you had resources. Ask first.

My power or gas is about to be cut

Phone before the cut-off date, not after. Reconnection costs money you do not have.

BC Hydro — electricity. Monday to Friday 7 am to 8 pm, Saturday 9 am to 5 pm

1-800-224-9376

FortisBC — natural gas

1-888-224-2710

Step one: a payment plan

Phone BC Hydro and ask to pay the balance in instalments. Anyone can ask. You do not have to be low income.

Ask about the Equal Payment Plan too. It spreads your bill evenly over 12 months so winter does not spike.

Step two: the Customer Crisis Fund

This is a grant. You do not pay it back.

  • Up to $800 if your home is heated by electricity, up to $700 if it is not.
  • You must be the residential account holder, for your main home.
  • Your payments must be at least 21 days overdue, or you are facing disconnection.
  • Your balance must be $1,000 or less, and you must have made some attempt to pay.
  • A life event in the last 12 months caused the crisis.
  • One grant per 12 months.

Apply online at BC Hydro, or on a paper form at any Service BC office. A decision usually takes about 21 days.

Apply before you owe $1,000

Above $1,000 you do not qualify. People wait for the disconnection notice and by then the balance is too big. Apply as soon as you are 21 days behind.

Winter is not automatic protection here

BC Hydro pauses disconnections from 1 November to 31 March — but not everywhere the same way.

On Vancouver Island and the Lower Mainland, you are only protected on days when the forecast average temperature for the next 24 hours is below 0 °C. In the rest of BC the pause is complete.

So in Victoria, winter alone will not keep your power on. Late charges keep adding up either way.

If someone in the home needs powered medical equipment

This matters if you use an oxygen concentrator, a ventilator, a feeding pump, dialysis at home, a powered bed or lift, or you charge a power wheelchair.

We looked for a written BC Hydro programme that protects these accounts — a medical register, a doctor's certificate that stops a disconnection. We could not find one published on BC Hydro's own customer pages, and we found none for FortisBC either. We are telling you that plainly rather than promising something that may not exist.

Here is what to do anyway. It works often enough to be worth every minute:

  1. Phone BC Hydro now at 1-800-224-9376, before the disconnection date. Say: "There is life-sustaining medical equipment in this home." Name the equipment.
  2. Ask them to put a medical note on the account and ask what protection that gives you. Write down the name of the person you spoke to and the date.
  3. Get a letter from your doctor or nurse practitioner saying you depend on the equipment. Ask if you can send it in. A letter in hand changes conversations.
  4. Ask about the Customer Crisis Fund and a payment plan in the same call. Clearing the arrears is the surest protection.
  5. Make a backup plan today. Charge every battery. Ask your equipment supplier about a backup battery or a manual method. Ask Island Health home care what happens if your power goes out.
  6. If the power is cut and someone's life depends on the equipment, call 911. That is what it is for.

If you have oxygen at home, tell your oxygen supplier as well. They plan for outages.

There is no BC Hydro-style crisis grant for gas that we could confirm. If your gas bill is the problem, phone FortisBC at 1-888-224-2710 and ask for a payment arrangement. FortisBC electricity customers, outside Victoria, use 1-866-436-7847.

Cold and storms: Extreme Weather Response

From 15 October to 15 April, extra overnight shelter spaces open in BC when a community declares an extreme weather alert — hard cold, snow or heavy rain.

They open only on alert nights, and only overnight. There is no single number that announces it. Phone 2-1-1 in the day, or ask any shelter or drop-in, to find out whether tonight is an alert night and where the extra spaces are.

If you use a wheelchair

We have to be straight with you about this one.

We went looking for which Victoria shelters are wheelchair accessible. We could not find that written down anywhere. Cool Aid's own shelter page names its three shelters and says nothing about accessible entrances, beds or bathrooms. One of the two directories that used to list Victoria shelters has gone offline, and its web address is now up for sale.

That does not mean no shelter is accessible. It means a wheelchair user cannot find out without phoning, which is exactly the wrong way round.

So phone, and ask these questions directly:

  • "Can I get in with a wheelchair — is there a step or a lift?"
  • "Is there a bed on the ground floor, or an accessible bed?"
  • "Is there a bathroom and a shower I can use in a chair?"
  • "Where can I charge a power chair overnight?"
  • "Can I keep my chair with me, or does it have to be stored?"

Start with Cool Aid at 250-383-1977 and 211. If nowhere can take you, say so out loud to whoever you reach — it is their job to keep looking, and it is a fact worth recording.

I cannot pay for my prescription

Do not skip doses while you sort this out. Take the pharmacy route first — it is the fastest.

  1. Go to your pharmacy and say you cannot pay today. Ask for an emergency supply. BC pharmacists can give you a short supply to keep you going when you cannot reach your prescriber. Ask them to bill it to PharmaCare.
  2. If you are on income, disability or hardship assistance, you are on Plan C. Plan C covers eligible drugs at 100%. There is nothing to apply for — the ministry enrols you. Tell the pharmacist you are on assistance and ask them to check Plan C.
  3. If you are not on assistance, register for Fair PharmaCare. It is free and you only do it once. Registering can wipe out your deductible if your income is low. Call 1-800-663-7100.
  4. For psychiatric medication, ask your doctor, nurse practitioner or psychiatrist about Plan G. It covers many psychiatric drugs at 100% for a year at a time. They fill in the form for you.
  5. If you have no doctor, the Cool Aid Community Health Centre serves people who are unhoused or on low incomes. Call Cool Aid at 250-383-1977.

I am about to lose my home

A 10 Day Notice gives you 5 days, not 10

If you get a 10 Day Notice to End Tenancy for unpaid rent, you have five days to either pay the rent in full or apply to dispute it.

If you do nothing in those five days, your landlord can get an order of possession without a hearing. Five days is the whole window. Phone today.

Two calls, in this order:

  1. TAPS, 250-361-3521, 828 View Street. Free advocates for evictions and for assistance problems. Walk in Monday, Tuesday, Thursday, Friday, 9 am to noon and 1 to 4 pm. Wednesday is phone only.
  2. Greater Victoria Rent Bank, 250-383-6166 extension 1. Emergency money to prevent an eviction, for people in Victoria, Saanich, Esquimalt, Langford, Sooke, Sidney, Oak Bay, View Royal, Colwood, the Highlands, Metchosin, Central Saanich, North Saanich, Salt Spring and the Southern Gulf Islands. You can apply online at bcrentbank.ca.

If you are on assistance, ask the ministry for a crisis supplement for shelter at the same time — the words are above. Bring the eviction notice.

The Residential Tenancy Branch handles disputes: 1-800-665-8779, Monday to Friday 9 am to 4 pm. Apply for dispute resolution there. Let TAPS help you do it if you can.
